Design one screen of a web game, "Orbital Books", a bookstore on a space station. The screen is "Decisions": the station's robot bosses brief the player, and the player picks moves for the next quarter on a requisition form. The output is a single finished UI mockup at exactly 1280x720 (16:9), seen flat, like a screenshot.

Use the attached assets as the actual art, not as loose inspiration:
- room.png: the backdrop, a dreary concrete server room. It is the whole page's background, full-bleed.
- boss-overall.png, boss-profit.png, boss-culture.png, boss-happiness.png, boss-readers.png: five robot bosses, transparent cutouts on one shared scale (keep their relative sizes and exact colours). Left to right in reading order: overall (a tall desk on treads with a clipboard), profit (a filing-cabinet robot), culture (a pale bust-headed figure), happiness (a floating drone), readers (a squat robot with a counter display).
- requisition-form.png: the look of the moves form (a dark laminated sheet, ruled boxes, a violet REQUISITIONED stamp on chosen moves). Reuse its style; it does not need its current size or place.

Requirements:
1. The robots are the star: LARGE, together filling about 55 to 65 percent of the screen height, standing on the room's floor. Nothing covers a robot's face or body.
2. A slim dark navy navigation sidebar on the far left, about 220 px wide, with a few menu rows. The rest is the scene, edge to edge.
3. Five small speech-bubble tags, one per robot, each with a name line and a short teaser line. One of them is the open one, lit in its robot's colour.
4. One expanded briefing panel for the open robot: a name, two or three lines of capitals (the joke), and a lighter strip of two or three plain lines (the facts).
5. The requisition form "Your moves (choose up to two)": three moves, each a tick-box, a bold title and a two-to-four-line description; one move ticked and stamped; a "Submit plan" button. All of it visible without scrolling at 1280x720.
6. Overlays sit in the scene's quiet areas (ceiling, upper wall, floor band, edges) as frosted dark glass or laminated panels, with enough scrim that all text is easily readable (WCAG AA) over the room.
7. Nothing moves when another bubble opens: panels have fixed places and sizes.
8. Palette: the room's muted cool greys with a navy tint, deep navy panels, near-white text, accents violet #6b5cff and soft red #ff7a90 used sparingly.
9. Text: use short, plausible English for headings and labels if you can render it cleanly; otherwise use neat grey bars. No logos, no emoji, no people.
