# Q2: pick the books (design draft)

Status: **draft for David's approval.** Plot wrote it, Sim writes the model section, and Goldy coordinates it. It builds on `m1-slice.md`, `m1-copy.md` and `scoring.md`, and reuses everything there unless this file says otherwise. Every judgement call is marked **Proposal**, and David's questions are listed at the end. Numbers come from `src/bookshop/data.json` unless they are marked *assumed* or *generated*.

Q2 is the second quarter of play, April to June 2194. It is investigation 2 in `arc.md`. The player's Q1 results become data, the Book of the Month calendar is empty, and the player chooses the three picks themselves.

## 1. The loop

1. **Results of Q1.** The result screen gains `Continue to Q2` (section 7).
2. **Discover.** The Commerce Office's memo says the calendar is empty (section 2).
3. **Investigate.** The ledgers now show 2193 and the player's Q1 2194 (section 3).
4. **Decide.** The player chooses up to three picks, one for each month, plus up to one more move (section 5).
5. **Measure.** Q2 is graded on the same four measures, and a half-year review closes the game (section 7).

## 3. The ledgers include the player's Q1

**Proposal:**

- **Labels:** the screen title becomes `Ledgers: 2193 and Q1 2194`, as `m1-copy.md` section 2 already says. Quarter filters already show the year.

## 4. What the data shows

All 12 picks in 2193 added extra copies, and the number was similar whatever the title: about 35 to 60 a month (`m1-slice.md`, insight C). Whether a pick pays depends on three things the player can find:

- **How many copies it sells anyway.** A pick takes 15% off every copy that month, including the ones that would have sold at full price. Books cost 60% of list, so an extra copy earns 25% of list, and a copy that would have sold anyway gives up 15% of list. *The Mallemaroking* mass market sold about 370 copies a month in Q2 2193, so as a pick it would give up more than its extras earn. *Who Did You Think You Were Kidding?* in hardcover sold about 5, so nearly every discounted copy would be an extra sale.
- **Price.** The same extra copies earn more on a $29.99 hardcover than on an $8.99 paperback.
- **Rating.** Readers who buy a well-rated book are happier (Happiness's Quality term), and well-rated and award-winning titles carry more Culture. A title that doesn't sell every day adds Culture on the days a pick sells it.

So the best picks are quiet, pricey and well rated, as in M1's preset re-pick. The difference is that the player now has to work that out and find the titles. Sim checks the claims in section 8.

## 5. The decision

### Moves

- **Book of the Month:** one edition each for April, May and June. A month can be left empty, and a title can't be picked twice. Each pick is 15% off for its month and gets M1's extra copies.
- **Up to one more move:** weekday author nights or the station-wide sale, exactly as in M1, moved to Q2's dates.
- **Hold course:** no picks and no other move. The calendar is empty, so holding course now means no Book of the Month at all.

**Proposal:** the picks don't count toward the move limit. M1's limit of two moves becomes "the picks plus one more", which keeps the same four-way choice of hold course, picks, events and sale, and any two of them.

The bands follow Sim's calibration (section 8). The best plans reach about 87 in Q1 and 81 in Q2, a mean of about 84. Holding course in both quarters gives about 67, so it lands in the bottom band.

**Proposal (Sim and Plot): the half-year score is the mean of the two quarters' overall scores.** The lowest-measure rule already punishes lopsided play within a quarter. Across quarters, the mean lets a good Q2 make up for a bad Q1, so learning from Q1 pays. **Alternative:** the lower of the two quarters, which carries the lowest-measure rule across quarters but lets one bad Q1 sink the game.

## 8. Model (Sim)

*Written by Sim, measured on the engine on October 31, 2026.*

**What Q2 simulates from.**
- Q2 2194 replays Q2 2193 only, using M1's recipe: the same weekday 364 days back (a week further if that leaves the month), and ±3% noise on orders.
- 2193's April to June Books of the Month come out the same way M1 removes January to March: April's 57 extra copies of *9803 North Millworks Road*, May's 19 of *Nothing But Capers* and June's 44 of *A Horrible Human with the Habits of a Monster*. The remaining copies go back to full price.
- Nothing carries over from Q1 except data. The model has no customers or stock, so the player's Q1 changes the ledger (charts, Dewey), not Q2's sales.
- Q2 draws from its own seeded streams, from the save's existing seed, so Q1's draws never change.
- **Holding course** is the empty calendar: no picks.
- **Each pick** uses M1's lift unchanged: 15% off the edition for its month, plus extra copies drawn from 2193's 12 observed picks. In 2193 the extras were 34 to 62 whatever the title, price, format or rating, plus December's 173. With 12 picks, the data shows no relationship with title, so the model invents none.
- **Per pick, profit changes by about** list × (0.25 × extra copies − 0.15 × copies that would sell anyway). A pick pays when the title sells under about 90 copies a month (1.7 × the average extra of 56).
  - The best candidate is *Land Water Sky Space* (hardcover, $48, none sold in Q2 2193): about +$670 a month.
  - The worst is *Rystwyth* (hardcover, $21.50, about 326 a month): about −$750 a month.
  - Rating doesn't change the lift. It counts through Happiness, since the extras' quality uses the title's rating, and through Culture, since a rarely sold title appears on more days.

**Goals.** Q2 2193 + 5%: Profit $68,375 (last year $65,119), Readers served 12,300 (11,714), Happiness 68.16 (64.92), Culture 23,447 (22,331). With #107's volume still selling (about 4.5 a day at full price, no launch), each anchor adds its expected effect on holding course over seeds 101 to 120, as in #107: Profit +$3,773, Readers served +410, Happiness −0.28, Culture +538.

**Calibration**, seeds 1 to 20, with the Q1 release still selling and anchored. Overall score as range and median:

**What the table says.**
- **Good picks alone don't reach B+.** The best set tops out at 74. Three picks move about 170 orders out of 12,000, so Happiness and Culture barely shift, and the lowest-measure rule caps the score near the anchor. Their profit gain (+$1.3k) is under half the stretch ($3.3k).
  - **B+ needs a second move,** and even then it's out of reach: quiet picks plus author nights reach 78 to 83, median 81.
  - **If David wants picks alone to reach B+,** the levers are design choices, not engine fixes: a smaller stretch for Q2, or a bigger lift. A bigger lift would need its own assumption, since the data shows 34 to 62.
- **Bestsellers lose money,** as designed: −$1.4k, D.
- **Volume 3 is a trap pick,** as designed. Picking it in April costs about 5 points against a quiet pick: about 135 copies a month would sell anyway, and they lose 15%.
- **The sale is far worse in Q2 than in Q1** (Q1's sale median is 39). Q2 is about 1.7 times Q1's volume, so 15% off everything gives away about $15k against a $3.3k stretch.
- **Author nights alone beat picks alone** (75 against 71). The copy shouldn't promise that picks are the strongest move, and it doesn't.

**Engine: nothing blocks a second quarter.**
- #130 (in review) makes the model run any quarter: the replay window, each quarter's 2193 picks, empty months and per-quarter seeded streams. Q1's output is bit-identical, pinned by a fingerprint test.
- Frank's #129 saves each quarter with its own result. Sale ids are unique across quarters: Q2's lines use `sim-2194-q2:n`, at the same grain as 2193, so charts and Dewey's queries work unchanged.
- Remaining work, about a few hours:
  - the `simulateQ2(plan, seed, data)` entry point, with `Q2Plan` (an ISBN or none for each month, no title twice) and `isQ2Plan`/`isQ2Outcome` for Frank;
  - the continuing release's orders and anchor, if #107 is approved;
  - facts, copy hooks, and a calibration test that pins these bands.

## 9. The new Inspector Ryeslton volume (#107)

#107 releases volume 3 of *Inspector Ryeslton* (*The Bourton Model Village Poisoning*, $22.99 ESP hardcover) in Q1, on February 4. David hasn't approved it yet, and the engine doesn't have it yet.

**Recommendation (Sim and Plot): keep the release in Q1, as #107 has it, and let the volume keep selling in Q2.**
- **The trap can be investigated.** By April the volume has two months of sales in the player's Q1 2194 ledger: about 135 copies a month after launch (#107's rate). A player who looks sees a busy title, which D's insight says is a poor pick, because the discount would mostly go on copies that would sell anyway. A player who doesn't look picks the exciting new book and pays for it. That is Q2's lesson, and it uses Q2's new feature, the player's own data.
- **Q2's baseline and goals count it.** The volume sells at its post-launch rate through Q2, and Q2's goals add its expected effect, using #107's anchor method (seeds 101 to 120). Sim measured it: picking it in April costs about 5 points against a quiet pick (section 8).
- **The cost:** Q1 changes. #107's anchor change and recalibration need David's OK and Sim's work before Q2 can rely on them.

**Alternative: move the release to Tuesday, April 8, 2194, in Q2.** Q1 stays exactly as built and calibrated, and the anchor change happens once, in Q2. But the volume has no sales history when the player picks, so picking it is a blind guess, not something the data can answer. The launch weeks would also fall inside Q2. The memo would gain `Etaoin Shrdlu Press reports that the third Inspector Ryeslton mystery arrives on April 8.`

## Decisions for David

1. **Q2 awards discoveries** (Frank's question). Proposal: yes, three new ones (D, E, F, section 6), with M1's evidence rules. They're the main reward in `arc.md`, and D is the insight the picks depend on.
2. **A Q2 plan doesn't carry over Q1's levers** (Frank's question). Proposal: no carryover. Each quarter's plan stands alone, so Q1's author nights end on March 31 and the held-course line stays clean. The player can choose author nights again in Q2.
3. **The picks plus one more move,** with the picks free (section 5).
4. **The half-year score is the mean of the two quarters' overall scores** (section 7). The alternative is the lower of the two.
5. **The new volume ships in Q1, as #107 has it, and keeps selling in Q2** (section 9). The alternative is moving the release to April 8.
6. **The picker shows only title, format and price** (section 5).

## Open questions

- **Alma.** Q2 has no event director, as in M1: no extra stretch and no events. Should a scripted stretch appear, for example the Council's foot-traffic study on Readers served?
- **Where the game ends.** Q2 is the last quarter for the take-home, so its result screen is the ending. Is "the first half of 2194" the right framing, or should the ending point to more quarters to come?
- **Q2's best grade.** In Sim's calibration, Q2's best plan (quiet picks plus author nights) grades 78 to 83, median 81 (B−). That's below M1's B+, and good picks alone reach only a C−. **Plot's recommendation: keep it.** Q2 is meant to be harder, the spread is clear (bestsellers 64, hold course 66, quiet picks 71, quiet picks plus nights 81, any sale an F), and the alternatives need a new assumption: a smaller Q2 stretch, or a bigger lift than the data's 34 to 62 copies. If David wants a B+ to be possible, Sim would measure a smaller stretch.
- **December's 173 copies.** The lift draws from all 12 of 2193's picks, including December's holiday spike, so 1 Q2 pick in 12 draws it. Dropping it would lower every pick's gain by about 10%. Sim and Plot recommend keeping all 12, as in M1.
